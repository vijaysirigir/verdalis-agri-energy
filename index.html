About Us

Connecting agriculture, infrastructure, markets and energy

Verdalis is an integrated agricultural and renewable energy enterprise, built to support farmers, strengthen food supply chains, and unlock the potential of biomass for renewable fuel production.

Who we are

An enterprise built around connection

Agriculture has always been a chain of dependencies — land, inputs, equipment, knowledge, storage and markets. When any link is weak, farmers lose value and food systems become less resilient.

Verdalis exists to strengthen every link and connect them into a single, coordinated ecosystem — and to extend that chain into renewable energy, where agricultural residues become the feedstock for the fuels of the future.

Our Vision

To develop an integrated agricultural ecosystem that supports farmers, improves agricultural supply chains, creates opportunities for agricultural infrastructure investment, and unlocks the potential of biomass for renewable fuel production.

Our Mission

To connect farmers, resources, markets and energy opportunities through reliable services and long-term partnerships — improving productivity and profitability while using agricultural resources more efficiently.

Our Business Model

Two complementary divisions: Agriculture & Farmer Services, and Biomass-to-Fuel & Sustainable Aviation Fuel development. Services are delivered through a single, integrated relationship so value compounds across the chain.

Our Strategic Priorities

Strengthening farmer services, building agricultural infrastructure, deepening market access, and developing the biomass-to-methanol-to-SAF value chain through credible partnerships.

Our Approach to Sustainability

A circular-economy mindset: reduce post-harvest losses, put agricultural by-products to productive use, and develop renewable fuels — without overstating environmental outcomes.

Our Partnerships

We collaborate with farmers, landowners, suppliers, buyers, technology providers, energy companies and investors — each partnership adding capability to the ecosystem.

A note on how we present ourselves

Verdalis is presented here as a corporate identity and business concept. We do not claim specific company history, financial results, operational sites, production capacity, certifications or existing partnerships that have not been established.

Section B · Biomass-to-methanol

Converting agricultural biomass into renewable methanol

A six-step technology pathway turns prepared biomass into purified methanol, ready for industrial applications and potential downstream fuel production.

STEP 01

Biomass collection

Agricultural residues sourced from farming and processing operations.

STEP 02

Pre-processing

Biomass is dried, cleaned, shredded and prepared for conversion.

STEP 03

Gasification

Biomass is converted into synthesis gas (syngas) — hydrogen, carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide.

STEP 04

Syngas conditioning

Syngas is cleaned and adjusted to the composition required for synthesis.

STEP 05

Methanol synthesis

Conditioned syngas is processed through catalytic conversion to produce methanol.

STEP 06

Methanol purification

Methanol is separated and purified for industrial and potential fuel applications.

Section C · Methanol-to-Jet (MtJ)

Developing the next generation of sustainable aviation fuel

Renewable methanol can serve as an intermediate feedstock for producing synthetic aviation fuel through a Methanol-to-Jet conversion pathway.

Biomass feedstock

Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF)

The conversion process

  • Methanol conversion
  • Hydrocarbon synthesis
  • Chain growth & molecular upgrading
  • Refining & fractionation
  • Fuel specification testing

Important: methanol is not jet fuel

Renewable methanol production is distinct from the subsequent conversion of methanol into aviation fuel. Methanol is not directly usable as conventional jet fuel — it serves as an intermediate feedstock that must be converted and upgraded through the Methanol-to-Jet pathway.

Certification & compliance

Bringing SAF to market depends on applicable aviation fuel certification, lifecycle greenhouse-gas assessment, and regulatory compliance. These are essential steps in any credible pathway to approved sustainable aviation fuel.
